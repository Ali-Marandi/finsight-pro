# FinSight Evidence — Accounting-Firm Market Research

## Demand signal

The Intuit QuickBooks Accountant Technology Survey 2025 reports that 79% of surveyed accountants expect strategic advisory work to increase, while 81% say AI has improved productivity. The same survey highlights an important product-design constraint: firms use an average of eight applications for core operations; integration difficulty, manual data entry, subscription cost, and staff training are recurring friction points. The GTM must therefore position FinSight as a narrow workflow layer that improves the current client-data path, not as another platform that requires replacing the stack.

The CPA.com/Blue J 2026 survey of more than 1,000 tax professionals reports weekly use of AI-powered tax research by 60% of firms, compared with 33% in 2025. It also shows that advisory projects, document analysis, and drafting are active AI workflow areas, while practitioners expect a movement toward value-based or hybrid billing. This supports a value proposition around proof-backed advisory deliverables, rather than hourly data cleanup.

## Adoption barrier and trust requirement

Thomson Reuters reports that 29% of accounting firms cite data quality and consistency as a main barrier to automation. The source identifies inconsistent client formats, sensitive PII, legacy formats, governance, and security as recurring data-management challenges. The product should lead with local-first processing, explicit data lineage, review gates, minimal data collection, and client-visible evidence—not an opaque AI claim.

## GTM implications

| Observation | FinSight action |
|---|---|
| Firms want more advisory revenue but have technology overload. | Sell one sharply defined workflow: "messy client files to reviewable advisory proof." |
| Data quality blocks automation. | Product-led demonstration must start with a prospect’s anonymized file and visibly expose a real data issue or reusable mapping. |
| Trust and data security matter. | Use local-first desktop pilot, no default training on client files, clear retention/deletion behavior, and reviewable audit trail. |
| AI is now familiar but credibility matters. | Promise evidence-backed suggestions and human confirmation; never promise autonomous accounting judgment. |
| Value-based billing is expanding. | Packaging should be per analyst and active client/proof workflow, aligning cost with advisory capacity instead of raw file volume. |
